# Assumptions and Key Decisions

Working document for the CSS Curriculum AI Transition Plan.
Status markers: **DECIDED**, **OPEN**, or **ASSUMPTION**.

Last updated: June 2026

---

## 1. Context and Scope

**A1 (assumption):** The decline in CS internship and entry-level hiring is structural, not cyclical. AI is permanently changing the composition of software engineering teams, reducing demand for undifferentiated junior developers while increasing demand for engineers who can work effectively alongside AI tools.

**A2 (assumption):** Graduates who can use AI as a force-multiplier will be more competitive than those who cannot, even for traditional roles.

**A3 (assumption):** Foundational CS skills - algorithms, systems, software engineering process, mathematics - remain essential. AI tools are not a substitute for understanding what the code does.

**A4 [DECIDED]:** AI fluency is a **core competency** required of all graduates, not a specialization track.
- *Implication:* A new required AI course (CSS 4xx) must appear in the CSSE required core and in the AC core. This is not achievable through a required elective alone. Space must be made by restructuring existing required courses.

**A5 (assumption):** The AC and CSSE programs need differentiated responses. AC students have more concentration flexibility; CSSE students need stronger required coverage.

## 2. Faculty and Resources

**A6 [DECIDED - Multiple approaches recommended]:** A faculty skills inventory is needed before finalizing what can be taught internally. Given the sensitivity of self-assessment and the risk of inaccuracy, the department should use several complementary methods:

**Method 1: Anonymous self-report survey** (Survey-Faculty-AIReadiness.md)
- Fastest; captures interest and willingness alongside capability
- Risk: self-assessment may not reflect actual teaching capability; some faculty may over- or under-report
- Best for: identifying professional development needs and volunteering

**Method 2: Syllabus and course content audit**
- Ask each instructor to share current syllabi for all their courses
- A small review committee (2-3 faculty) reads for AI-related content, methods, and tools
- Reveals what is already happening vs. what is self-reported
- Best for: identifying who is already integrating AI without fanfare

**Method 3: Research and publication review**
- Review faculty CV and recent publications/presentations for AI/ML content
- Can be done by chair or assistant without faculty involvement
- Best for: identifying faculty with deep research expertise who may not think of themselves as "AI teachers"

**Method 4: One-on-one conversations with department chair**
- 30-minute structured conversation with each full-time faculty member
- Questions: what AI topics could you teach now? What would you need to teach more? What are you excited about?
- Most accurate for gauging genuine capability and enthusiasm
- Cost: significant chair time; ~20-30 hours for full faculty

**Method 5: Structured teaching demo or workshop participation**
- Invite faculty to a hands-on AI bootcamp (see A7); observe who engages substantively vs. superficially
- Low-pressure; not evaluative; but reveals genuine engagement level
- Best for: practical capability assessment

**Recommendation:** Combine Methods 1 (survey), 4 (chair conversations for full-time faculty), and 5 (bootcamp observation). Method 2 (syllabus audit) can be done concurrently at low cost. Method 3 is a quick supplemental check.

**A7 [DECIDED - Context established]:**
- Teaching track faculty: 8 courses/year standard load
- Tenure track faculty: 5 courses/year standard load
- Approximately 50% of courses are taught by adjuncts
- Course releases for full-time faculty are difficult even with funding, because adjunct supply for replacement teaching is not guaranteed
- Two funding scenarios are planned for: **Moderate** and **Generous** (see Section 7)

*Implication:* New required AI courses will likely need to be covered by a combination of (a) reassigning new hire teaching capacity, (b) qualified adjuncts with AI industry experience, and (c) faculty who voluntarily integrate AI into courses they already teach. Mandating AI content redesign without any course release support is unrealistic.

**A8 [DECIDED - Context established]:**
- 3 new teaching track faculty + 1 new tenure track faculty start September 2026
- Additional retirements are anticipated; net faculty count remains stable (new lines replace departing faculty)
- *Opportunity:* Incoming faculty represent a significant capacity injection for AY 2026-27 and beyond. If any of the 4 new hires have AI/ML teaching expertise, they could anchor the CSS 4xx new course. The department should make AI teaching capacity an explicit criterion in all future hiring.

## 3. Academic Governance and Timeline

**A10 (assumption):** Modifying existing course content can be done by the instructor with minimal formal approval, as long as credit hours and learning outcomes stay the same.

**A11 (assumption):** Creating a new course number requires curriculum committee approval, typically 1-2 quarters from submission to approval.

**A12 (assumption):** Changing a course from elective to required requires formal approval and is subject to catalog year rules.

**A13 [DECIDED]:** Changes to required courses apply to **new incoming students only**. Currently enrolled students may opt in to the new requirements by request to advising.

**A14 - DROPPED:** ABET accreditation concerns are noted but will not be used as a planning constraint in this proposal.

## 5. Curriculum Change Philosophy

**A20 [DECIDED]:** Both modification of existing courses and new courses are required.
- **Short term (2026-27):** Modify existing courses to weave in AI content
- **Medium term (2027-28 or later):** Bring in new AI courses
- Existing course modifications lay the groundwork; new courses provide depth

**A21 - subsumed by A4:** Since AI is a core competency, an AI-area course will be required (not just elective). CSS 382 (modernized) and the new CSS 4xx serve different roles (see A22).

**A22 [DECIDED]:** A new standalone course "AI for Software Engineers" (CSS 4xx) is the right vehicle - not a modification of CSS 360 or CSS 370. A new course avoids curriculum baggage and can be designed from scratch for its specific purpose.
- CSS 382 (Intro AI) will be modernized in parallel but serves a different role: conceptual foundations
- CSS 4xx serves a different role: practical integration and deployment

**A23 [DECIDED]:** Eliminating low-enrollment and outdated courses is on the table. The schedule data confirms several courses are effectively dormant (never offered or not offered in 2025-26). Course elimination frees teaching capacity, catalog space, and student cognitive load when reading requirements.

---

## 6. What We Cannot Assume

**A24:** We cannot assume students will self-study AI tools outside the curriculum.

**A25:** We cannot assume the AI landscape is stable. New required courses must teach principles and frameworks, not specific tools.

**A26:** We cannot assume all faculty are willing or able to integrate AI quickly. Change management and support are needed.

**A27:** We cannot assume adjuncts for new AI courses are readily available. Industry practitioners with AI expertise may be available but need to be identified and vetted in advance.

## 7. Funding Scenarios

Two scenarios are used to plan Phase 1 and 2 initiatives. Both assume the 4 new faculty positions starting Sept 2026.

### Moderate Funding Scenario
Available resources beyond normal operations:
- Summer stipends for 2-3 faculty to develop or revise course content ($3,000-5,000/faculty)
- 1 course release in AY 2026-27 for the CSS 4xx lead developer (replaces with adjunct if available, otherwise overload pay for adjunct teaching)
- Budget for faculty AI professional development: $5,000-10,000/year (workshops, conference, tools)
- AI tool institutional access for courses: $2,000-5,000/year (GitHub Copilot Education, API credits)
- Guest lecturer honoraria: ~$4,000/year (8 guests at ~$500 each)

Under this scenario:
- 1-2 existing courses can be significantly redesigned per year (with stipended faculty)
- CSS 4xx can be developed by one lead faculty member with protected time
- Faculty bootcamp can be a 1-day event with 1-2 external facilitators
- New AI elective courses depend on new hire expertise (no extra capacity for existing faculty without overload)

### Generous Funding Scenario
Available resources beyond normal operations:
- Summer stipends for 4-6 faculty: $3,000-5,000 each
- 2-3 course releases per year across multiple faculty for major course overhauls
- Dedicated AI curriculum development budget: $20,000-30,000/year
- AI compute and tool resources: $10,000/year (including cloud API credits for student labs)
- Adjunct hiring budget specifically for AI-experienced industry practitioners: 2-4 adjunct courses/year
- Faculty AI bootcamp: 2-day residential event with external facilitators and industry guests
- Conference travel: $3,000-5,000/faculty for AI education conferences (2-3 faculty/year)

Under this scenario:
- 3-5 courses can be significantly redesigned per year
- CSS 4xx can have a primary instructor and a co-instructor (reducing single-instructor dependency)
- A second new AI course can begin development in 2027-28
- Qualified AI-experienced adjuncts cover new sections while full-time faculty focus on course development

### Zero/Minimal Funding Scenario (contingency)
If no additional funding is approved:
- Phase 1 modifications proceed on voluntary basis (faculty goodwill, no compensation)
- CSS 4xx development relies entirely on new hire capacity (if applicable) or a faculty member absorbing it into normal load
- Faculty bootcamp becomes a half-day internal workshop run by existing faculty
- Adjunct AI expertise sourced from existing network only
- Phase 2 timeline slips 1-2 quarters
