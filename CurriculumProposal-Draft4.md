## Executive Summary

The Computing & Software Systems Department has decided that **AI fluency is a core competency** required of every graduate of both undergraduate degrees (CSSE BS and Applied Computing BA). Not a specialization, not an elective track: a requirement. Today a student can complete either degree without taking a single AI course, and every AI course we do offer sits in a thin elective layer that depends on one or two individual instructors.

The department has a three-phase plan to fix this between now and 2028-29:

- **Phase 1 (2026-27):** Integrate AI content into five existing required courses and the capstone rubrics; pilot new content through special topics; run a faculty AI bootcamp. No curriculum committee action needed.
- **Phase 2 (2027-28):** Launch CSS 4xx, "AI for Software Engineers," as a new required course; deploy a modernized CSS 382; remove 8-11 dormant courses from the catalog. Requirement takes effect for new admits Autumn 2028.
- **Phase 3 (2028-29+):** AI present at every level from CSS 142 through capstone, with a stable two-course AI pathway.

**The request:** a two-year investment of approximately **$99,000-149,000** under the recommended (Generous) scenario, or **$37,000-63,000** at the Moderate floor, plus the Dean's backing on two non-monetary items: AI teaching capacity as an explicit criterion in future CSS faculty searches, and support for the catalog cleanup. A funding decision by mid-September 2026 lets Phase 1 start on time with the new academic year.

Four new faculty (3 teaching track, 1 tenure track) start in September 2026. Their AI teaching capacity, confirmed during onboarding, is the single most important variable for how far the budget stretches.

## 1. Why Now

The entry-level software job market has shifted. AI tools now do a substantial share of the work that used to go to junior engineers, and employers have raised the bar accordingly: they expect new graduates who can use AI as a force-multiplier and critically evaluate what it produces. We think this shift is structural, not cyclical. Graduates without these skills will compete for a shrinking pool of traditional roles.

Our current curriculum does not deliver these skills. Six years of schedule data (Autumn 2020 through Winter 2026) show the problem concretely:

- **No AI content is required.** All AI-area courses are electives. A student can graduate from either degree without any of them.
- **The elective layer is fragile.** CSS 382 (Intro AI) is the healthiest, with 3 instructors and 2-3 sections per year. The others each depend on one instructor: our machine learning course missed an entire year, our computer vision course was absent from the schedule for two full years, and our NLP course has been offered exactly once. If one person leaves or changes focus, an entire content area disappears.
- **The catalog carries dead weight.** Eight courses in the catalog have never been offered at UWB in the data window, and three more have gone dormant. They mislead students and advisors about what we actually teach.

Peer departments are moving. Few have yet made AI a required competency for all graduates. Doing it now, and doing it visibly, is a recruitment and placement argument for UWB, not just a curriculum fix.

## 2. What the Department Has Decided

These decisions are made and recorded in the project's working documents:

1. **AI fluency is a core competency** required of all CSSE and AC graduates.
2. **A two-track response.** Modernize CSS 382 (conceptual foundations: how AI works) and create a new CSS 4xx, "AI for Software Engineers" (practical integration: building, evaluating, and responsibly deploying software with AI components). Both are needed; they serve different purposes.
3. **CSS 4xx will be required**, appearing in the CSSE required core and the AC core.
4. **Sequencing:** modify existing courses first (2026-27), launch new courses second (2027-28). Integration lays the groundwork; the new course provides depth.
5. **Catalog cleanup is in scope.** Dormant courses will be formally removed.
6. **New students only.** Requirement changes apply to new admits (Autumn 2028 and later); current students may opt in through advising.

## 3. The Plan in Three Phases

### Phase 1: 2026-27, Integration Without Restructuring

Every CSS student encounters AI meaningfully this year, with no new course numbers and no curriculum committee action. The work:

- **Five required courses add AI modules.** CSS 360 (Software Engineering) adds a two-week module on AI-assisted development and team AI-use policies; this is the highest-priority change, since CSS 360 is where most students first do real team development. CSS 211 (Computers and Society) refreshes its AI ethics content with post-2022 cases. CSS 301 (Technical Writing) adds writing about AI systems: model cards, disclosure, communicating uncertainty. CSS 350 (Management) adds IP, disclosure, and QA questions raised by AI in the workflow. CSS 343 (Data Structures II) adds light conceptual framing connecting its material to modern AI.
- **Capstone rubrics change now.** Starting Autumn 2026, every CSS 497 and CSS 496 project documents an AI feasibility analysis and an AI tool-use log.
- **Special topics pilots.** One or two CSS 490/390 offerings in Winter or Spring 2027 pilot the CSS 4xx content before it has a permanent number.
- **CSS 382 revision begins.** Reduce legacy symbolic AI coverage; add ML foundations, transformer architecture at a conceptual level, LLMs and their failure modes, and hands-on work with real libraries and APIs. Revised syllabus by Spring 2027.
- **Faculty development.** A faculty AI bootcamp in late September 2026 before the quarter starts, a monthly learning community through the year, and course development stipends for the faculty doing the redesign work.

### Phase 2: 2027-28, Structural Addition

- **CSS 4xx launches.** 5 credits, prerequisite CSS 343 with CSS 382 recommended. Six units: AI as a software component, prompt engineering and LLM interaction, retrieval-augmented generation, agents and orchestration, evaluation, and responsible deployment. Teams build and evaluate a working AI-integrated application as the final project. Proposal to the curriculum committee March 2027; pilot offering Autumn 2027; required for new admits Autumn 2028.
- **Modernized CSS 382 deploys** Autumn 2027, giving students a coherent pathway: CSS 342/343, then CSS 382 (theory), then CSS 4xx (applied).
- **Catalog cleanup executes.** Eight never-offered courses removed; three near-dormant courses removed or retained after confirmation with their instructors.

### Phase 3: 2028-29+, Goal State

AI literacy at every level: introductory courses frame AI tools critically from week one, the required pathway is stable with at least two instructors per AI course, and every capstone includes AI feasibility analysis. Optional deeper electives (ML, vision, NLP, bias) hang off the required pathway as a visible, advisable track.

## 4. One Pending Internal Decision

Adding a required 5-credit course without removing one creates degree bloat. The faculty will vote in Autumn 2026 between two options:

- **Option C (recommended):** CSS 370 (Analysis and Design) moves from required to strongly recommended elective. Its content overlaps most with CSS 360 and CSS 350, and it stays in the catalog: it runs 7 sections a year and will keep running. The freed 5 credits become the CSS 4xx slot.
- **Option D (backup):** restructure CSS 350 and CSS 360 into a new two-course sequence. Feasible but harder: two new course proposals, 10+ instructors to coordinate, and disruption to two of our highest-volume courses.

**Why the funding request does not wait on this vote:** the CSS 4xx course itself, the Phase 1 integration work, the CSS 382 revision, and the faculty development program are identical under either option. The vote only determines which slot the requirement occupies. Approving the budget now keeps the timeline; the committee package in March 2027 will reflect whichever option the faculty choose.

## 5. What We Are Asking For

### 5.1 Funding

Two scenarios, built around actual constraints: teaching track faculty carry 8 courses a year, tenure track 5, roughly half our courses are adjunct-taught, and course releases are hard to arrange even when funded because replacement adjuncts are not guaranteed.

**Phase 1 (2026-27):**

| Item | Moderate | Generous |
|---|---|---|
| Course development stipends | 2-3 faculty at $4,000 = $8,000-12,000 | 4-6 faculty at $5,000 = $20,000-30,000 |
| Course release (CSS 4xx developer) | 1 release; adjunct coverage ~$6,000-8,000 | 2 releases; ~$12,000-16,000 |
| Faculty AI Bootcamp (Sept 2026) | 1 day, ~$3,000-5,000 | 2 days, ~$8,000-12,000 |
| AI tool institutional access | $3,000-5,000/yr | $8,000-12,000/yr |
| Guest lectures | ~$4,000/yr | ~$8,000/yr |
| **Phase 1 total** | **~$24,000-34,000** | **~$56,000-78,000** |

**Phase 2 (2027-28):**

| Item | Moderate | Generous |
|---|---|---|
| Course development stipends | 1-2 faculty at $4,000 = $4,000-8,000 | 3-4 faculty at $5,000 = $15,000-20,000 |
| AI-experienced adjunct sections | 1-2 courses = $6,000-16,000 | 2-4 courses = $12,000-32,000 |
| AI tool access | $3,000-5,000/yr | $10,000/yr |
| Conference travel | None or 1 faculty | 2-3 faculty at $3,000 = $6,000-9,000 |
| **Phase 2 total** | **~$13,000-29,000** | **~$43,000-71,000** |

**Two-year total: Moderate ~$37,000-63,000. Generous ~$99,000-149,000.**

Estimates are rough orders of magnitude for planning; actuals depend on adjunct arrangements, tool choices, and participation.

**Recommendation: fund the Generous scenario.** The marginal money buys the things that make the change durable rather than fragile: a second CSS 4xx instructor by 2028-29 (removing the single-instructor dependency that plagues our current AI electives), 3-5 course redesigns per year instead of 1-2, and a pipeline of AI-experienced industry adjuncts. Moderate delivers the plan on the same timeline but leaves resilience thin.

**What no funding costs:** Phase 1 proceeds on faculty goodwill only, CSS 4xx development depends entirely on new-hire capacity or unpaid overload, and the Phase 2 timeline slips 1-2 quarters. The plan survives, slower and more fragile.

### 5.2 Non-Monetary Asks

1. **Hiring criterion.** Endorse AI teaching capacity as an explicit criterion in all future CSS faculty searches. The September 2026 cohort was hired before this plan; future searches should not be.
2. **Catalog cleanup backing.** Removing 8-11 courses will require curriculum committee and advising coordination. The Dean's visible support shortens that process.
3. **A decision by mid-September 2026.** The bootcamp, the stipends, and the course release all need to be committed before autumn quarter starts.

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| New hires have no AI teaching expertise | Medium | Confirm during September onboarding; if the gap is real, redirect adjunct budget to an AI-experienced practitioner for CSS 4xx |
| AI tools and practices change faster than courses can | High | Teach principles and frameworks, not products; use CSS 490 for fast-moving content |
| Curriculum committee approval delayed | Medium | Submit March 2027 with full documentation; pilot via CSS 490 in parallel |
| Single-instructor fragility persists | High if unfunded | Generous scenario funds a second CSS 4xx instructor by 2028-29; cross-train two instructors per AI course |
| Course release cannot be covered by an adjunct | Medium | Identify a qualified adjunct in early autumn; fall back to compensated overload |
| Students lack personal AI tool access | Medium | All course content uses free-tier or institutionally licensed tools; the tool-access budget line exists for exactly this |

## 8. What Success Looks Like

By 2028-29, a CSS graduate can:

1. Use AI coding assistants effectively and critically evaluate their output
2. Integrate a cloud AI API into a software system with appropriate error handling and evaluation
3. Explain conceptually how transformer-based LLMs work and where they fail
4. Identify and analyze bias in an AI system and document its risks
5. Articulate the legal and ethical responsibilities of an engineer deploying AI
6. Argue from evidence when AI is the right approach and when it is not

Measurable along the way: all five targeted required courses carrying AI modules by end of Phase 1; 100% capstone rubric adoption from Autumn 2026; CSS 4xx approved, piloted, and required on schedule; the catalog smaller by 8-11 entries; and student and employer survey deltas between the Autumn 2026 baseline and 2028.
